\def\abstracttext{%
%	Non-interactive zero-knowledge (NIZK) proof systems are often constructed based on cryptographic assumptions. In this paper, we propose the \emph{first} unconditionally secure NIZK system in the $\aczero$-fine-grained setting. More precisely, our NIZK system has perfect soundness for all adversaries and unconditional zero-knowledge for $\aczero$ adversaries, namely, an $\aczero$ adversary can only break the zero-knowledge property with negligible probability unconditionally. At the core of our construction is an OR-proof system for satisfiability of 1 out of polynomial many statements.
In this paper, we study multi-party non-interactive key exchange (NIKE) in the fine-grained setting. More precisely, we propose three multi-party NIKE schemes in three computation models, namely, the bounded-parallel-time, bounded-time, and bounded-storage models. The bounded-parallel-time and bounded-time constructions rely on mild fine-grained assumptions, whereas the bounded-storage construction is unconditional. This improves the recent work of Afshar, Couteau, Mahmoody, and Sadeghi (EUROCRYPT 2023) that requires idealized assumptions, such as random oracles or generic groups.

Additionally, we show that all our constructions satisfy a natural desirable property that we refer to as extendability, and we give generic transformations from extendable multi-party NIKEs to multi-party identity-based NIKEs in the fine-grained setting.

Furthermore, we generalize the lower bound on users' storage consumption in the bounded-storage model by Dziembowski and Maurer (Eurocrypt 2004) to encompass any multi-party NIKE with extendability and local key independence. Together with our construction, this lower bound shows that the users' storage consumption of our multi-party NIKE in the bounded-storage model is optimal up to constant factors within this natural class.
}
